# Candidates — Funniest Lost Sock Status Update

Five lines were written from the `humor-one-liner` skill (punchline in the
last words, a named person plus a specific time, a specific + absurd +
vivid premise, never explain the joke, steer clear of the dryer /
missing-partner cliché). The leaderboard was still blind when these were
built (0 items), so no current winner could be scanned; the pick leans on
the skill and on the 16 finals of 26 Nov 2026 (our lines scored 328k–681k
against rank 1 at 747k–895k — we lost on specific/absurd).

O1 originality check: the chosen line's signature phrase ("dietary
choice" as a sock punchline) was searched verbatim on the web — 0 verbatim
hits; what came back was generic vet pages about dogs eating socks, none
of them containing this line. No candidate matches byte-for-byte any line

## Chosen (candidate 1) — 105 chars

> Lost sock update: I'm inside Rex now. He ate me Tuesday. He keeps telling the vet I'm a 'dietary choice.'

Why it takes the slot: the quoted punchline sits in the last words
("dietary choice"); Rex is named and Tuesday is pinned down; the premise
is specific, absurd and vivid (a status update filed from inside the dog);
the joke is never explained; and the dryer / missing-partner cliché is
sidestepped entirely by putting the sock somewhere it should never be.
The sock's voice is small, domestic and weary, which fits the deadpan
judge brief. Checks C1–C9: a single quote segment only, at the end (no
early leak); setup:punchline ≈ 2.5:1; every concrete noun in the punchline
is new.

## Rejected

### Candidate 2 — 116 chars

> Update: I've been living behind the washing machine since Tuesday. My twin just got promoted to dishcloth. I peaked.

Rejected: "I peaked" is a shrug, not a laugh-turn — it restates defeat
instead of reversing the premise; and "behind the washing machine" is one
step away from the dryer cliché the judge explicitly penalises. The
twin-promoted-to-dishcloth beat is the strongest image here, but it is
spent in the middle, not at the end.

### Candidate 3 — 111 chars

> Day 12 under the car seat. Dave finally noticed I'm gone — he bought a replacement pair. They don't even match.

Rejected: the ending is an observation, not a reversal — nothing flips in
the last words, so it earns a smile, not a laugh (criterion 2 fails). It
also leans on the missing-partner trope without twisting it far enough:
the replacement pair is the expected beat, and "they don't even match" is
the first association anyone would write.

### Candidate 4 — 115 chars

> I've been in the couch since March. My owner just sat on me and said 'finally, lumbar support.' I've been promoted.

Rejected: two punchlines fight each other — the quote in the middle
("finally, lumbar support") spends the laugh before the final pivot
(anti-pattern AP-5), and "I've been promoted" then lands as an echo rather
than a twist. One punchline, at the very end, beats two diluted ones.

### Candidate 5 — 119 chars

> Day 47 in the lost-and-found box. My partner got claimed by a jogger. I'm still here, holding a raffle ticket for 2019.

Rejected: the ending is a detail, not a reversal — a raffle ticket for 2019
is specific and absurd, but it sighs instead of flipping the premise. And
the lost-and-found box is the most generic lost-sock location there is, so
the line opens exactly where a hundred other entries open.
